ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Food waste is one of the most pressing challenges faced by the global food
supply chain. In retail environments, a significant proportion of perishable
goods are discarded due to poor demand prediction, inadequate environmental
monitoring, and absence of any proactive logistics strategy. This project
presents the "Intelligent Food Waste Optimizer," an end-to-end AI-powered
platform designed to minimize food waste in retail supply chains.

The system integrates two distinct Machine Learning models — Facebook Prophet
for time-series demand forecasting and a Random Forest Classifier for spoilage
risk detection — with a real-time Route Optimization engine. Environmental
factors such as temperature, humidity, and rainfall are analyzed alongside
product-specific shelf-life data to generate accurate 7-day spoilage risk
forecasts. When a product is flagged as high-risk, the system automatically
computes the most efficient delivery route to nearby NGOs and food banks using
the Haversine distance formula.

The platform features a full-stack architecture: a Flask-based RESTful backend
serving ML predictions, and a React.js frontend offering an interactive
dashboard, demand charts, spoilage risk curves, and an NGO dispatch interface.
The system supports 10 diverse product categories including Vegetables, Dairy,
Bakery, Meat, Fruits, and Beverages. Evaluation results demonstrate that the
system can successfully identify high-risk products and recommend optimized
donation routes, thereby converting potential food waste into a meaningful
social impact.


================================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 Background
--------------
Globally, approximately one-third of all food produced for human consumption
is lost or wasted each year, totalling around 1.3 billion tonnes. This waste
occurs across all stages of the food supply chain — from farms and processors
to retailers and consumers. Within the retail sector, perishable goods such as
vegetables, dairy, meat, and bread are particularly vulnerable to spoilage due
to environmental conditions and inaccurate demand forecasting.

Traditional inventory management systems rely on manual inspections and static
expiry dates to determine the fate of products. These approaches are reactive
in nature — they act upon spoilage rather than predicting and preventing it.
Moreover, surplus stock that could be donated to NGOs or food banks often goes
to waste simply because no structured logistics system exists to redirect it
in time.

1.2 Motivation
--------------
The motivation behind this project stems from two key observations:

(a) Predictive Analytics Gap: Retailers lack affordable, data-driven tools
    that can forecast demand at the product level and also assess spoilage
    risk based on live environmental conditions.

(b) Logistics Vacuum: Even when surplus food is identified, the absence of
    an automated routing system means that redistribution to NGOs depends
    entirely on manual decision-making, which is too slow to act on
    short shelf-life products.

This project addresses both gaps with a unified intelligent platform.

1.3 Objectives
--------------
The primary objectives of this project are:

1. To develop a machine learning model capable of forecasting weekly product
   demand using historical sales data.

2. To build a spoilage risk prediction model that evaluates environmental
   conditions to generate a 7-day risk progression curve for each product.

3. To implement a Route Optimization engine that identifies high-risk products
   and maps the most efficient donation routes to nearby NGOs.

4. To deliver a responsive, full-stack web application that integrates all
   three modules into a seamless user experience.

5. To validate the system using synthetic but realistic data modeled on
   real-world retail scenarios.

1.4 Scope of the Project
------------------------
This project covers the following scope:
- Demand forecasting for 10 product SKUs across 6 categories.
- 7-day spoilage risk prediction with daily granularity.
- NGO route optimization using geographical coordinates.
- Export of reports in CSV format for offline analysis.
- Smart product search with partial name matching.

The system does not currently integrate with live weather APIs or real POS
(Point of Sale) systems, though these are identified as future enhancements.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
================================================================================

2.1 Food Waste in Retail Supply Chains
---------------------------------------
According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), retail-level food
waste accounts for approximately 5% of total food loss globally. Studies by
Gustavsson et al. (2011) highlight that in developed economies, consumer and
retail-level waste is disproportionately high compared to production-level
losses. The primary causes identified are overproduction, poor inventory
management, and lack of dynamic pricing or redistribution mechanisms.

2.2 Demand Forecasting Using Machine Learning
----------------------------------------------
Time-series forecasting has long been applied to retail inventory management.
ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average) models were traditionally used
for this purpose. However, their assumption of linear relationships limits
their effectiveness for data with strong seasonal patterns.

Taylor & Letham (2018) introduced Facebook Prophet, a decomposable
additive model designed specifically for time-series data with multiple
seasonalities. Prophet handles missing data, outliers, and holiday effects
natively, making it well-suited for retail sales forecasting. This project
adopts Prophet as the primary demand forecasting engine.

2.3 Spoilage Prediction Using Environmental Data
-------------------------------------------------
Studies by Li et al. (2019) demonstrate that produce spoilage is strongly
correlated with temperature and humidity levels. Machine learning classifiers
such as Decision Trees and Random Forests have been successfully applied to
classify food freshness based on sensor readings.

Breiman (2001) introduced the Random Forest algorithm as an ensemble learning
method that reduces overfitting by averaging predictions across multiple
decision trees. Its ability to handle high-dimensional, non-linear data makes
it ideal for multi-factor spoilage classification.

2.4 Logistics and Route Optimization
--------------------------------------
The Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) is a well-studied optimization challenge
in logistics. For the purpose of NGO food donation routing, the primary metric
is geographic distance. The Haversine formula, derived from spherical
trigonometry, provides accurate great-circle distance calculations between
two GPS coordinates and is widely used in location-based services.

2.5 Summary of Research Gap
----------------------------
While individual solutions exist for demand forecasting, spoilage detection,
and route optimization separately, no integrated platform has been identified
in the reviewed literature that combines all three in a single, accessible,
open-source web application targeted at small-to-medium retail businesses.
This project fills that gap.

3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

3.1 Overview
------------
The system follows a three-tier architecture:

  [User / Browser]
       |
  [React Frontend]  <-->  [Flask Backend API]  <-->  [ML Models & Data Layer]

The frontend communicates with the backend exclusively via RESTful HTTP
requests. The backend loads pre-trained ML models at startup and serves
predictions in real time.

3.2 Component Breakdown
------------------------

(a) Data Layer:
    - daily_aggregated.csv: Primary dataset containing 90 days of historical
      sales, environmental, and shelf-life data for 10 products.
    - ngos.json: A JSON database of nearby NGOs with name, address,
      geographical coordinates, capacity, and accepted food categories.

(b) Machine Learning Layer:
    - demand_model_P00X.pkl: Trained Prophet models, one per product.
    - spoilage_model.pkl: A trained Random Forest Classifier.

(c) Backend (Flask):
    - /predict/demand  [POST]: Returns 7-day demand forecast.
    - /predict/spoilage [POST]: Returns 7-day spoilage risk curve.
    - /predict/route   [GET]: Returns high-risk items and NGO routes.
    - /health          [GET]: Returns server and model health status.

(d) Frontend (React.js):
    - Dashboard: High-level KPIs and spoilage alerts.
    - Demand Forecast: Per-product 7-day demand chart.
    - Spoilage Risk: Per-product 7-day risk curve.
    - Route Optimization: NGO dispatch interface with export.

3.3 Data Flow
-------------
1. User selects a product on the frontend.
2. Frontend sends a POST request to the Flask backend.
3. Backend loads the pre-trained model from cache.
4. Model generates a prediction based on the product's environmental data.
5. Backend applies a time-decay factor and returns JSON response.
6. Frontend renders the results as an interactive chart.

4. METHODOLOGY

4.1 Dataset Description
------------------------
The primary dataset (daily_aggregated.csv) contains the following fields:

  - product_id      : Unique identifier (P001 to P010)
  - product_name    : Human-readable name (e.g., Fresh Tomatoes)
  - date            : Date of the record (DD/MM/YYYY)
  - units_sold      : Number of units sold that day
  - avg_price       : Average selling price per unit (in INR)
  - temperature     : Ambient temperature (°C)
  - humidity        : Relative humidity (%)
  - rainfall_mm     : Daily rainfall in millimetres
  - category        : Product category (Vegetable, Dairy, Bakery, etc.)
  - shelf_life_days : Product-specific shelf life in days

The dataset spans 90 days with 90 records per product, totalling 900 rows.
Environmental data was synthetically generated to reflect realistic Indian
climatic conditions.

  P004 - Full Cream Milk  (Dairy,     Shelf Life: 5 days)
  P005 - Carrots          (Vegetable, Shelf Life: 6 days)
  P006 - Bananas          (Fruit,     Shelf Life: 5 days)

  P010 - Greek Yogurt     (Dairy,     Shelf Life: 4 days)

4.2 Demand Forecasting Model (Prophet)
---------------------------------------
Framework: Facebook Prophet (Additive Decomposition Model)

The forecasting model uses the following decomposition:

  y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + ε(t)

Where:
  g(t) = Trend component (long-term growth/decline)
  s(t) = Seasonal component (weekly patterns)
  h(t) = Holiday effects
  ε(t) = Error term

One Prophet model is trained per product using 90 days of historical
units_sold data. At inference time, the model generates a 7-day future
forecast. Predicted dates are remapped to start from the current date,
ensuring the output is always forward-looking and relevant.

4.3 Spoilage Risk Prediction Model (Random Forest)
----------------------------------------------------
Algorithm: Random Forest Classifier (Scikit-learn)
Features Used: Temperature, Humidity, Rainfall, Shelf Life Remaining

Training Labels:
  - Label = 1 (High Risk): temperature > 30°C AND shelf_life_days <= 2
  - Label = 1 (High Risk): humidity > 75% AND shelf_life_days <= 3
  - Label = 0 (Low Risk): All other cases

Final Risk Score Formula:
  Final Risk = min(1.0, (P_ML × 0.6) + Time_Factor + 0.05)

Where:
  P_ML        = Probability of class 1 from Random Forest (0 to 1)
  Time_Factor = (day_index / 7.0) × 0.4
  0.05        = Constant base risk for all perishables

This formula combines the ML model's environmental assessment with a
deterministic time-decay component, ensuring that risk increases daily as
the product ages, even if environmental conditions remain constant.

4.4 Route Optimization
-----------------------
Algorithm: Haversine Distance Formula

When a product's 7-day average risk exceeds the dispatch threshold (45%),
it is flagged for donation. The system then calculates the distance from the
central store to each NGO in the database using the Haversine formula:

  a = sin²(Δφ/2) + cos(φ1) × cos(φ2) × sin²(Δλ/2)
  d = 2 × R × atan2(√a, √(1−a))

Where:
  φ = Latitude (in radians)
  λ = Longitude (in radians)
  R = Earth's radius (6,371 km)
  d = Distance in kilometres

NGOs are ranked by proximity (distance) and a suitability score is computed
as: Suitability = max(0, 100 − (distance × 5))

The top 2 nearest NGOs are recommended per high-risk product.

5.3 Key Implementation Details
-------------------------------
(a) Model Pre-loading:
    All 10 demand models and the spoilage model are loaded into memory at
    server startup using a demand_models_cache dictionary. This eliminates
    cold-start errors on the first user request.

(b) Case-Insensitive Product Resolution:
    All product IDs are normalized to uppercase (P001, P002) before model
    lookup, preventing "model not found" errors from case mismatches.

(c) Consistent Risk Calculation:
    A shared calculate_spoilage_metrics() function is used by both the
    /predict/spoilage and /predict/route endpoints, ensuring that risk
    scores are identical across all pages of the application.

(d) Date Remapping:
    Prophet forecasts are generated from the training data's cutoff date.
    The predicted demand values are extracted and their dates are remapped
    to start from the current date, so users always see future-looking data.

(e) Partial Name Search:
    The resolveProductInput() function on the frontend accepts partial names
    (e.g., "Milk", "Bread", "Chicken") and resolves them to the correct
    product ID before sending the API request.

6. RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
================================================================================

6.1 Demand Forecasting Results
--------------------------------
The Prophet model successfully generates 7-day demand forecasts for all 10
products. Sample output for P001 (Fresh Tomatoes):

  Date          Predicted Units
  2026-03-07    147
  2026-03-08    134
  2026-03-09    118
  2026-03-10    132
  2026-03-11    137
  2026-03-12    124
  2026-03-13    115
  Total 7-Day Demand: ~907 units

The model captures weekly seasonality patterns, reflecting higher demand
on weekdays and slightly lower over weekends for some products.

6.2 Spoilage Risk Results
--------------------------
The spoilage model generates a 7-day risk progression curve. As expected:
- Products with shorter shelf lives (P002 - Spinach: 2 days) show
  significantly higher average risk scores (~50%)
- Products with longer shelf lives (P009 - Orange Juice: 14 days) show
  consistently low risk scores (~8-12%)
- Risk increases day-over-day due to the time-decay factor in the formula

6.3 Route Optimization Results
--------------------------------
Products with an average risk score exceeding 45% are flagged for dispatch.
In a typical run, P002 (Spinach Bunch) appears as the primary candidate.

Sample Output:
  Product: Spinach Bunch (P002) | Risk: 50.0% | Quantity: 50 units

  Recommended NGOs:
  1. City Food Bank        | 2.3 km | Match Score: 88.5%
     Address: 12 MG Road, Bengaluru
  2. Hope Community Kitchen| 4.1 km | Match Score: 79.5%
     Address: 45 Residency Rd, Bengaluru

6.4 System Performance
-----------------------
- Backend API response time: < 500ms per request (after model warm-up)
- Model pre-loading time at startup: ~8-12 seconds (all 10 demand models)
- Frontend render time: < 200ms (React with Recharts)
- CSV export: Instantaneous (client-side generation)

7. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This project successfully demonstrates the design and implementation of an
intelligent, data-driven platform for minimizing food waste in retail
environments. By combining demand forecasting, spoilage risk detection, and
route optimization into a unified web application, the system addresses both
the prediction and action aspects of the food waste problem.

Key achievements of the project include:
1. Accurate 7-day demand forecasts using Facebook Prophet for 10 product SKUs.
2. A deterministic-plus-ML hybrid spoilage risk model that produces consistent,
   realistic, and product-specific risk scores.
3. An automated NGO routing engine using the Haversine formula, capable of
   identifying optimal donation destinations in real time.
4. A full-stack web application with smart product search, live autocomplete,
   CSV export, and a clean responsive UI.
5. A self-contained dataset covering 10 products across 6 food categories.

The platform demonstrates that AI-driven waste reduction tools can be built
affordably and accessibly, without requiring enterprise-level infrastructure.
